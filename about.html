About & methodology

← back to national dashboard

This is the public, institution-level dashboard's methodology page. No individuals are named in any public output.

Faculty Adequacy measures the gap between the faculty a US medical school lists in its public directory and the faculty who show observable teaching, research, or clinical evidence in public records. Each directory-listed person is placed in one of five ordinal tiers:

  • Tier A — teaching-evidenced: named as a course / clerkship / thread director in current (≤24-month) published curriculum documents.
  • Tier B — basic-science: preclinical / basic-science faculty classified as teaching-oriented.
  • Tier C — research-active: ≥3 first/last-author papers per year, or ≥$250k active NIH direct costs, or a named institute/center directorship.
  • Tier D — clinically-active: an active NPI with a practice location near a campus and a specialty matching the department.
  • Tier E — no evidence found: none of the above appears in the public sources checked (the "ghost roster" signal).

How the data is gathered

Guiding principle: agents build programs; programs do the work. Large language models are used only to generate the per-school directory scrapers and to read curriculum documents; everything else — cross-referencing, scoring, and tiering — is deterministic Python. Each directory name is cross-referenced against public sources:

DirectoriesEach school's public faculty directory (roster of names, titles, departments). NPPESNational Provider Identifier registry — clinical practice, location, specialty. NIH RePORTERActive federal research grants and direct-cost amounts. PubMed / OpenAlex / ORCIDPublication record and authorship position over time. CurriculaPublished course/clerkship/thread listings — the only source that earns Tier A.

Records are matched across sources by name + affiliation with conservative entity resolution (a suffix mismatch — Jr/Sr/II/III — is never merged). The pipeline is idempotent and resumable; every figure here is a read-only snapshot of the current database.

How complete is this?

174 of 204 configured US medical schools currently have a banked faculty roster (≥20 records) — about 85%. Those rosters hold 122,133 directory faculty, of whom 109,020 are tier-classified.

122 of 174 schools with data have a public curriculum source curated (70%) — Tier A is only measurable for those. At the other 52, a Tier A of zero means not measured, not "no teaching faculty".

Coverage grows as directory-URL discovery and extraction improve; the remaining schools are mostly blocked on hard-to-crawl directories (search-only portals, JS/SPA rosters, anti-bot walls) rather than on the classification itself.

Known issues & limitations

  • Tier A measures curriculum-document coverage as much as it measures faculty. Tier A is earned only by being named in a public curriculum document, and that document has to be located and curated per school first. Only 122 of 174 schools with data currently have such a source; the other 52 show Tier A as not measured. Curating one more curriculum URL raises the national Tier A count with nothing having changed at any school — the count has jumped sharply on exactly that basis — so Tier A must never be compared between a school that has a curriculum source and one that does not.
  • The correlation explorer is gated, and its NIH axis is not an institutional total. The “what correlates with adequacy?” scatter on the dashboard plots only the 38 of 174 schools that clear a reliability gate (≥100 directory records, ≥50% evidence coverage, ghost-rate confidence interval ≤ 15 pp); the other 136 are hidden unless you ask for them, because a 20-record single-department scrape produces a “0%” that looks like a finding. NIH dollars on that chart are active awards flowing through the directory faculty we matched, not the institution's NIH portfolio. LCME and DO rosters differ structurally — DO rosters are small and preclinical, LCME rosters carry thousands of clinical affiliates — so compare within an accreditor, never across.
  • Provisional & incomplete. This is an experimental pipeline. Rosters marked known-incomplete at the source yield lower-bound ratios — the true Tier-A/B share can only be higher.
  • Coverage gaps. Some schools aren't in yet because their directory is a search-only portal, a JavaScript/SPA roster (e.g. VIVO research portals), or behind an anti-bot wall. Those show as missing, not as zero.
  • Single-department URLs. A directory URL that points at one department can undercount a school until the whole-school roster is discovered.
  • External-source coverage. Tiering depends on how consistently a person appears in PubMed / NIH RePORTER / NPPES under their name & affiliation. Inconsistent records can under-tier someone who is genuinely active — which is exactly what the per-person "how to raise it" steps address.
  • Entity resolution. Name-based matching can occasionally mis-merge or mis-split near-identical names.
  • Not a quality judgment. Tiers reflect what public federal/scholarly sources currently show, not the quality of anyone's teaching or the institution's instruction.
